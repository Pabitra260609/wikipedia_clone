Artificial intelligence (AI) is the intelligence of machines or software, as opposed to the intelligence of living beings, primarily humans. It is also an active field of study in computer science that develops and studies intelligent machines capable of performing tasks typically requiring human intelligence.

Overview

The field of artificial intelligence was founded in 1956 at a workshop held at Dartmouth College. In the decades since, research has explored varied methodologies including symbolic logic, knowledge-based expert systems, and modern data-driven statistical learning.

Modern artificial intelligence systems rely heavily on large datasets, high-performance computing hardware, and algorithms capable of identifying patterns and making predictions without explicit step-by-step programming.

Types of artificial intelligence

Artificial intelligence is commonly classified by its level of capability:

  • Narrow AI (Weak AI): Systems designed to solve a single, specific task, such as playing chess, filtering spam email, or recognizing faces. All current real-world AI systems fall into this category.
  • General AI (Strong AI / AGI): Theoretical systems that would possess the ability to understand, learn, and apply knowledge across any intellectual task at or above the human level.

Key subfields and techniques

AI includes multiple interconnected disciplines within computer science:

  • Machine Learning (ML): Algorithms that automatically improve through experience and exposure to data.
  • Deep Learning: A subset of machine learning using multi-layered artificial neural networks inspired by biological neural structures.
  • Natural Language Processing (NLP): Techniques that enable computers to process, understand, and generate human language.
  • Computer Vision: Systems that extract meaningful information from digital images and video feeds.

Applications

AI technologies are integrated across many areas of modern life:

  • Healthcare: Assisting in radiological analysis, disease detection, and drug discovery.
  • Transportation: Powering autonomous navigation, collision detection, and traffic management systems.
  • Consumer tools: Providing web search indexing, digital voice assistants, and personalized content feeds.
